# A Storm in the Night

The next day the sky turned grey before noon. By evening the wind had begun to howl through the valley, bending the trees and rattling the shutters of every house.

Meera climbed the hill anyway. The keeper was waiting at the door of the tower, and without a word he led her inside.

The stairs were steep and dark. At the top there was a small round room with windows on every side, and in the middle stood the great lamp. Its glass was polished so clean that Meera could see her own face in it.

"Watch," said the keeper. He struck a match, and the wick caught. The light grew and grew until the whole room was filled with gold.

Outside, the rain had started. It came down in heavy sheets, and the valley below disappeared into the darkness.

## The Travellers

"Look there," said the keeper, pointing towards the mountain road.

At first Meera saw nothing. Then she noticed a tiny point of light, moving slowly along the edge of the cliff. Then another, and another.

"Travellers," the keeper said quietly. "Merchants coming home from the city. On a night like this, the road is dangerous. They cannot see the edge. But they can see this lantern, and they know that the village lies beneath it. So they walk towards the light, and they stay on the road."

Meera pressed her hands against the cold glass. She watched the little lights move, step by careful step, closer and closer to the valley.

"Every night?" she asked.

"Every night," said the keeper. "Most nights, nobody needs it. But I never know which night will be the one that matters. So I light it every time."

***

The storm lasted until morning. The keeper and the girl stayed in the tower, adding oil to the lamp and wiping the mist from the windows, until the last of the travellers had reached the village below.
