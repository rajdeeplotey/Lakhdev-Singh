# The Morning After

When the sun rose, the valley looked washed and new. Puddles shone in the road, and the trees dripped with silver drops.

Down in the village, the merchants were unloading their carts. Some of them looked up at the tower and lifted their hands in thanks. The keeper waved back.

Meera was tired, but she felt something she could not quite name. It was the feeling of having done something small that was also important.

"Will you teach me?" she asked. "How to trim the wick, and clean the glass, and keep the oil from running low?"

The keeper looked at her for a long moment. Then he nodded.

## Many Years Later

The seasons turned, and the years went by. The old keeper grew older still, and one day he could no longer climb the stairs at all.

But the lantern did not go dark.

Every evening, when the sun went down behind the far mountains, a young woman climbed the narrow stairs and lit the great lamp at the top. The children of the village gathered at the bottom of the hill and counted the windows as the light passed each one.

And sometimes, one child would stay behind, sitting on a flat rock, looking up at the light and wondering who it was for.

Meera always came down to answer.
