# The Hill Above the Village

On the hill above the village there stood a small stone tower, and in the tower there lived a lantern keeper. Every evening, when the sun went down behind the far mountains, he climbed the narrow stairs and lit the great lamp at the top. Its light could be seen from every house in the valley.

The keeper was not young. His hands were slow, and his knees complained on the stairs. But he had kept the lantern for forty years, and he had never once let it go dark.

The children of the village liked to watch him. In the late afternoon they would gather at the bottom of the hill and count the windows of the tower as the light passed each one. First the lowest window glowed, then the middle one, and at last the top. When the lamp was lit, they cheered and ran home for supper.

One of those children was a girl named Meera. She did not run home with the others. She stayed, sitting on a flat rock, and looked up at the light for a long time.

One evening the keeper noticed her. He came down the hill slowly, with his lamp still warm in his hand.

## A Question

"Why do you stay?" he asked.

Meera thought about it. "I want to know why the light matters," she said. "There are no ships here. There is no sea. Who is the lantern for?"

The keeper smiled. It was a good question, and nobody had asked it in many years.

"Come back tomorrow," he said, "and I will show you."

***

That night Meera lay awake in her bed. Through the small window she could see the lantern on the hill, steady and golden, like a star that had decided to stay close to the ground.

She fell asleep wondering who else, somewhere in the dark, was looking at the same light.
