# The First Letter

The letter arrived on a Tuesday, in a pale blue envelope with a stamp from a country Arjun had never visited. His name was written on the front in careful, old-fashioned handwriting.

He opened it at the kitchen table, while the kettle began to whistle.

"Dear Arjun," it began. "You do not know me, but I knew your grandfather very well. Many years ago, he and I made a journey together along the great river. I have kept a promise to him for a long time, and now I would like to keep it with you."

Arjun read the letter twice. Then he turned off the kettle, which had been whistling for some time.

## The Promise

The letter explained that his grandfather had once left something behind, in a small town at the mouth of the river. He had asked his friend to make sure it reached his family one day.

"I am too old to travel now," the letter said. "But if you will come to the river, I will tell you where to look. Take the ferry from the northern bank. Ask for the ferryman called Daud. He will know who you are."

At the bottom of the page, there was a small drawing of a boat with a single sail.

***

That evening, Arjun took down the old photograph album from the top shelf. Near the back, he found a picture he had never paid much attention to. Two young men stood on a wooden pier, laughing at something outside the frame. Behind them, a boat with a single sail rested on the water.

He looked at the photograph for a long time. Then he went to find his travel bag.
