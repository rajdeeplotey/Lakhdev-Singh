# Home

The town at the mouth of the river was small and bright, with houses painted in faded blue and yellow. Daud led Arjun through narrow streets until they reached a house with a green door.

An old woman opened it. She was very small, and she held herself very straight. When she saw Arjun, she put her hand to her heart.

"So you came," she said. "I wrote the letter."

## What Was Left Behind

Inside the house, on a shelf above the window, there was a wooden box. The old woman took it down and placed it in Arjun's hands.

He opened it carefully. Inside, there was a bundle of letters tied with string, a small brass compass, and a notebook with a cracked leather cover.

"Your grandfather wrote in that notebook every day of our journey," the old woman said. "He wanted his grandchildren to read it, so that they would know where they came from. He made me promise to keep it safe until one of you came looking."

Arjun opened the notebook. The handwriting was the same as on the blue envelope, only younger and more hurried. The first page said: "For whoever reads this next. The river is long, but it always reaches the sea."

***

That night, Arjun sat by the window of the little house and read the notebook from beginning to end. Outside, he could hear the river meeting the sea, the way it had for a thousand years.

When he finally closed the book, the sky was turning pale. He took out a sheet of paper and began to write a letter of his own.
