# The Ferryman

The river was wider than Arjun had imagined. On the northern bank, a crowd of travellers waited beside a wooden landing, with bags and baskets and a goat that would not stand still.

The ferry was an old boat with a patched sail. At the back stood a tall man with a grey beard and a calm, patient face.

"Are you Daud?" Arjun asked.

The man studied him for a moment. Then he smiled, and his whole face changed.

"You have your grandfather's eyes," he said. "Come. Sit at the front. The river is gentle today."

## Crossing

The boat moved slowly across the brown water. Fishermen called to each other from small canoes, and white birds followed the ferry, hoping for scraps.

"Your grandfather crossed here many times," Daud said, guiding the rudder with one hand. "He always sat exactly where you are sitting. He said it was the best place to think."

"What did he think about?" Arjun asked.

Daud laughed. "Everything. The weather. The fish. Whether the world was round or only looked round. And, most of all, his family. He talked about them on every crossing."

Arjun watched the far bank come closer. He realised that he knew very little about his grandfather as a young man. He had only known him as an old man with a soft voice, who told stories that seemed too good to be true.

"Were the stories true?" he asked.

Daud was quiet for a while. The sail filled with wind, and the boat leaned gently.

"More true than you would believe," he said at last. "You will see."
